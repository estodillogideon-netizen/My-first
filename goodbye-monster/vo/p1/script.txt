In this town, never say goodbye.
Because the fog is listening.
There's a monster in it. It takes anyone who says that word. And nobody ever comes back.
So nobody here says it. Not ever.
We wave. We say "See you." Or we just walk away.
There are signs on every corner. No goodbyes.
At school, we practice safe words. "Catch you later." "Until next time."
I'm Milo. And I know the rule better than anyone.
Three years ago, at the bus stop, my mom hugged me.
And she whispered one word. "Goodbye."
The fog came in so fast. Two lantern eyes. A sound like the ground breathing.
And then she was gone.
Since then, I don't talk about her. Not to anyone.
Except Pia. My best friend. She's loud, she's fearless, and she never goes anywhere without her flashlight.
Last night, I found something in Mom's old box. Her voice recorder.
I pressed play.
"Milo, if anything happens to me, go to the lighthouse."
Pia didn't even blink. "Tonight," she said.
At midnight, we snuck out. The town was silent. The fog was thick.
We passed the hospital. In a window, an old man sat beside his wife's bed.
She was very sick. He held her hand.
And he whispered, "Goodbye, my love."
The lights flickered. The fog poured in through the window.
Two lantern eyes opened in the dark.
Pia clapped her hand over my mouth before I could scream.
When the fog was gone, so was he.
We ran. All the way to the cliff. All the way to the lighthouse.
The door was open. Inside, the fog was so thick we could barely breathe.
Pia's flashlight found a shape. Then another. Then hundreds.
People. Standing perfectly still. Frozen, like statues.
The old man from the hospital. The baker who disappeared last winter.
And in the middle of them all, my mom.
She was still wearing her blue scarf. Her eyes were closed. But she was breathing.
They weren't gone. They were kept.
I touched her hand. It was cold. She didn't wake up.
Then the ground started breathing again.
The lantern eyes rose up behind us.
And I understood. The fog only lets you in one way.
Pia grabbed my arm. "Milo, don't."
I looked at my mom. Then at my best friend.
"Goodbye, Pia."
