Last time. In my town, saying goodbye gets you taken by the fog.
I found my mom frozen in the lighthouse. And I said the forbidden word.
"Goodbye, Pia."
The fog wrapped around me like a cold blanket. And everything went quiet.
When I opened my eyes, I was inside it. A gray world with no sound.
All around me, people stood frozen. Mid-wave. Mid-hug. Mid-word.
And there she was. Mom.
I held her hand. Her fingers twitched. But she couldn't wake up. Not alone.
Outside, Pia was alone in the lighthouse. And she didn't run.
She found a dusty book on the stairs. The mayor's diary.
Forty years ago, Mayor Grimble had a daughter. Her name was June.
One morning, June packed a suitcase. She said goodbye at the bus stop. And she never came back.
The mayor never said goodbye back. He just stood there. For days.
His sadness got so heavy, it turned into fog.
The monster wasn't hunting us. It was holding on. So no one could ever leave him again.
Pia ran through the silent streets, banging on every door.
"Wake up! They're alive! Everyone is alive!"
Lights came on. One house. Then ten. Then the whole town.
And at the very end of the line, holding a lantern, was the mayor.
They all climbed the cliff to the lighthouse.
The fog roared. The lantern eyes burned.
Pia stood in front of everyone and took a deep breath.
"On three. One. Two. Three."
"Goodbye!" The whole town shouted it. All at once.
The monster couldn't hold all of us. It grew. It shook. It cracked like ice.
And then it burst into a million sparkling drops of light.
Inside, my mom's hand squeezed mine. Her eyes opened.
"Milo?" she whispered. "You got so big."
All around us, people woke up. Hugging. Crying. Laughing.
The mayor sat alone on the lighthouse steps, holding an old photo of June.
For the first time in forty years, he whispered, "Goodbye, sweetheart."
The last bit of fog floated up to the stars. And he smiled.
The next morning, the signs came down. All of them.
At the bus stop, Mom knelt down and fixed my scarf.
"Goodbye, Milo," she said.
And I smiled. "See you tomorrow."
Turns out, saying goodbye isn't what takes people away.
Never saying it is.
