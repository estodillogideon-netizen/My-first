1Our Goal

Our school store (tindahan) needs containers to store and sell snacks like candies, biscuits and chips. We designed two containers:

  • Container A: a rectangular box (rectangular prism) made of cardboard.
  • Container B: a round can (cylinder) made of cardboard with a lid.

We measured each one, computed how much material it needs (surface area), how much it can hold (volume / capacity), and how much it costs to make in Philippine pesos (₱).

9Reflection

✏️ You can tap the text below and edit it in your own words.

This task showed me that math is not only for the classroom. To make a good container for our tindahan, I needed to measure carefully, because a small mistake in centimeters changes the surface area and volume a lot.

Surface area told me how much cardboard to buy, so it is connected to money. Volume told me how many snacks the container can hold. I learned that two containers can use almost the same material but hold different amounts, so choosing the right shape saves money.

Using trigonometry, I found the longest item that can fit inside and its angle. This is useful when packing things like straws or skewers.

In real life, store owners, packaging designers and engineers use these same formulas to save materials, lower costs and reduce waste. Next time, I will measure two times and compare more shapes before deciding.
