In my town, every time you laugh, you float a little higher.
Most people bob an inch or two. Me? I'm Tomo. I laugh at everything. So I'm basically always up here.
That's why Grandpa walks me to school with a rope tied to my backpack.
Grandpa is the heaviest man in town. Not because he eats a lot. Because he never laughs. Not once. Not ever.
So every morning, I try.
Funny faces. Nothing.
A rubber chicken. Nothing.
The joke about the cow with no legs? Ground beef. Nothing. He just tugged the rope and said, "Walk."
My best friend Rika and I even made a plan. Operation Giggle.
We put a whoopee cushion on his chair. He sat on it. Looked at us. Said, "Excuse me." Nothing.
Rika says he's made of stone.
But every night, he still checks the rope twice before I fall asleep.
Then came my cousin's birthday party.
Cake. Balloons. And a clown.
The clown slipped on a banana, landed face first in the cake, and his wig flew into the punch bowl.
And I lost it.
I laughed so hard, the rope snapped.
Grandpa grabbed for it. He missed.
I shot up past the balloons. Past the roof. Past the birds.
Down below, everyone was shouting.
The firefighters brought their tallest ladder. Too short.
Mom tied three kites together. Too short.
Rika threw me her jump rope. Way, way too short.
My shoes fell off. I watched them fall all the way down.
A bird landed on my head and looked at me like I was the weird one.
Up here it was cold, and quiet, and my whole town looked like a toy.
I knew how to come down. Just stop laughing.
But I was so scared that I kept giggling. Nervous giggles. And every giggle took me higher.
I couldn't even see Grandpa anymore.
Then, from all the way down there, I heard it.
A huge, creaky, rusty laugh. Like an old door opening for the first time in years.
Grandpa was laughing.
And he was flying straight up.
He caught my hand right below a cloud.
"Grandpa, you laughed!"
"Don't tell anyone," he said. Then he laughed again, and we went even higher.
That's when he told me.
He used to laugh more than anyone. Grandma was the funniest person in town. When she was gone, he just stopped.
"I wanted to stay on the ground," he said. "Close to her."
"So how do we get down?"
He held me tight. "Slowly. Together. Breathe."
In. Out. My giggles went quiet. His laugh turned into a smile.
And we drifted down. Past the birds. Past the roof.
Grandpa pointed at a little rooftop. "That's where Grandma and I had our first picnic." He was still smiling.
Past the balloons. Right into the cake.
Everyone cheered. The clown cried.
Grandpa still walks me to school with the rope.
But now it's tied to both of us.
Because in my town, laughing makes you float.
And Grandpa says it's okay to fly, as long as you don't fly alone.
