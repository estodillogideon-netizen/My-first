In my world, on your twelfth birthday, you get one text message. From yourself, twenty years in the future.
My best friend got, "Buy the red bike." My cousin got, "Never eat gas station sushi."
Mine just said, "Don't open the door."
No name. No reason. Just that.
That night, at exactly midnight, someone knocked.
Three slow knocks.
Through the peephole, there was only a tall shadow, standing in the rain.
I didn't open it.
The next night, same time, same three knocks.
Every night, for a whole month.
My little sister Ellie thought it was a game. She'd whisper, "Is the door man back?"
And three slow knocks? That was her knock. The way she knocked on my bedroom door, every time she had a nightmare.
I made her promise. "Never open that door. Ever."
Then one night, I fell asleep on the couch.
I woke up to the sound of the lock clicking.
Ellie was standing in the open doorway.
And she was smiling.
The shadow stepped into the light. A tall, tired man, soaking wet.
He had my face. Just older.
It was me.
He dropped to his knees and hugged her so tight.
He was crying.
"I'm sorry," he whispered. "I just wanted to hear you laugh one more time."
That's when I understood. In his world, Ellie was gone.
He never wanted to come in. He just wanted to be close to her knock.
He started to fade, like fog in the morning.
He looked at me, and said one thing.
"Saturday. The lake. Don't let go of her hand."
Then he was gone.
On Saturday, at the lake, I held Ellie's hand all day. She said I was being weird.
I didn't let go once.
That night, my phone buzzed.
The old text had changed.
Now it said, "She's safe. Thank you."
