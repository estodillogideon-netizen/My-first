In my town, every kid gets a mask on their twelfth birthday. They say when you wear it, anyone who wants to hurt you glows red.
On my birthday, Grandma tied the fox mask on my face. I walked into school... and the whole school glowed.
My teacher. My classmates. The lunch lady. All red.
I ran home. And my mom glowed brightest of all. She made my favorite curry. I didn't touch it.
Only one person never glowed. Haru. My best friend since we were five.
So I stopped talking to everyone. Except Haru. We ate lunch alone on the roof, just the two of us.
Then the accidents started.
A flowerpot smashed right where I'd been standing. My bike brakes snapped on the hill. Somebody wanted me hurt. And everyone around me was red.
Haru put his arm around me. "Everyone's red, Ren. You can only trust me."
The night before the lantern festival, I went to Grandma's workshop. Hundreds of masks on the walls, watching me.
I told her everything. She laughed. Then she stopped.
"Who told you red means danger? Red is the color of the heart. The mask shows everyone who would get hurt to protect you."
The whole school. My mom. All of them... would protect me.
"And the ones who don't glow," Grandma whispered, "are the ones you watch."
Haru. The one who never glowed. The one who was always there when the accidents happened.
The festival filled the whole town with lanterns. Haru pulled my arm. "Come on. The best view is under the lantern tower."
I looked up. The ropes were cut.
The tower cracked... and fell, right on me.
But they were already running. My classmates. My teacher. My mom. Every red glow in town.
They grabbed me and pulled me out, just in time.
And Haru just stood there. Not running. Not glowing. I asked him why.
He took off his mask. "Everyone glows for you, Ren. Nobody ever glows for me."
"Nobody... except you."
Then his mask turned black.
