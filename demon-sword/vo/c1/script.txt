In this town, when the blue comet passes, two people swap bodies for one night. And last night, I swapped with a girl who doesn't exist.
I woke up with long hair... in my own bedroom. But everything was wrong.
Old wallpaper. A radio instead of a phone. A calendar from twenty-five years ago.
On the desk, a notebook. "Mira Hale. Age twelve."
Her little brother jumped on the bed. "Mira! Pancakes!"
I spent the whole day as her. Her school. Her friends.
Then the comet faded... and I was back in my body.
I searched everywhere. No Mira Hale. Anywhere.
So I wrote her a note and hid it under the loose floorboard in my room.
The next comet night, I swapped again. And under the same floorboard... was her answer.
The paper was yellow. Old. "Who are you? And why is your writing in my floor?"
We wrote every week. She became the best friend I never met.
Then one note scared me.
"My brother Leo keeps sneaking to the frozen lake at night. He wants to see the comet."
Leo.
My mom had a little brother named Leo. He fell through the ice when she was twelve. On comet night.
Mom never talks about him.
Mom's name before she got married... was Mira Hale.
The girl in the floor was my mom.
The last comet night came. I woke up in her body. Leo's bed was empty.
I ran. Through the snow. Down to the lake.
He was in the middle of the ice, looking up. Then it cracked.
I jumped in. Freezing. I grabbed his hand and pulled.
We lay in the snow, under the comet, breathing.
Then everything went white.
I woke up in my bed. It smelled like pancakes.
At the table sat a man I'd never seen. "Morning, kid," said Uncle Leo.
Mom looked at me, crying. She slid an old yellow note across the table.
"I waited twenty-five years to meet you."
Then she whispered, "The comet comes back tonight. And when I was in your body... I saw what happens to you."
